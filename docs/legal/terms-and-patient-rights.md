2. Nature of Clinical Diagnostics & Investigative Boundaries


2.1 Technical and Investigative Nature of In-Vitro Assays
Diagnostic laboratory evaluations represent an in-vitro analytical assessment of a biological specimen (such as whole blood, serum, plasma, urine, stool, fluid aspirates, or swabs) captured at a discrete point in time. Laboratory findings reflect analytical parameters observed under standardized operating procedures. They do not constitute an exhaustive or permanent determination of an individual's complete medical or physiological status.


2.2 Non-Establishment of a Doctor-Patient Relationship
The generation, issuance, or electronic delivery of a diagnostic report by Sawariya Diagnostic Lab does NOT establish a doctor-patient relationship between you and the Laboratory, its consulting pathologists, or its technical staff.
Our laboratory reports provide objective, analytical investigational data intended strictly for the clinical evaluation of a registered, qualified medical practitioner (physician or surgeon). Laboratory results must always be clinically correlated with physical examinations, detailed medical history, presenting symptoms, current pharmacotherapy, and complementary diagnostic modalities (e.g., ultrasonography, radiography, ECG). Numerical values falling outside biological reference intervals do not automatically establish active disease, nor do values within standard ranges entirely rule out underlying pathology.


2.3 Absolute Exclusion of Emergency Intervention
NEITHER THIS WEBSITE, NOR OUR DIGITAL INTAKE PORTALS, WHATSAPP DISPATCH HANDOFFS, TELEPHONE LINES, OR MOBILE PHLEBOTOMY UNITS ARE CONSTITUTED, EQUIPPED, OR LICENSED TO PROVIDE EMERGENCY TRIAGE, TRAUMA RESCUE, OR CRITICAL CARE SERVICES. IF A PATIENT EXPERIENCES ACUTE CHEST PAIN, SEVERE RESPIRATORY DISTRESS, UNCONTROLLED BLEEDING, ACUTE NEUROLOGICAL DEFICITS, SUSPECTED ANAPHYLAXIS, OR LOSS OF CONSCIOUSNESS, CEASE INTERACTION WITH OUR SERVICES IMMEDIATELY AND CONTACT LOCAL EMERGENCY MEDICAL SERVICES OR PROCEED TO THE NEAREST HOSPITAL EMERGENCY DEPARTMENT.

3. Diagnostic Risk, Biological Variation & Analytical Limitation Matrix


Biological specimen testing is subject to physiological rhythms, analytical measurement uncertainty, and pre-analytical interference. The following matrix details the technical and biological variables governing all laboratory determinations:


Risk Category
	Clinical & Operational Description
	Patient Obligation & Laboratory Standard
	Pre-Analytical Interference
	Non-compliance with 10–12 hour fasting protocols (e.g., Lipid Profiles, Fasting Blood Sugar), strenuous exercise, acute emotional stress, alcohol intake, or active consumption of prescription drugs, supplements (e.g., high-dose Biotin), or over-the-counter medications can alter biochemical and hormonal values.
	The Patient must strictly observe fasting and intake instructions and disclose all active medications, supplements, and clinical constraints to the phlebotomist prior to specimen collection.
	Inherent Biological Variation
	Human biochemical, hematological, and hormonal markers fluctuate naturally based on circadian rhythms, posture during draw, hydration levels, and menstrual phases.
	Serial trend evaluation by a treating physician is clinically superior to isolated single-point determinations. Minor numerical variations within or near reference margins must be correlated clinically.
	Specimen Integrity Rejection
	Biological samples may suffer in-vitro red blood cell rupture (hemolysis), excessive lipid turbidity (lipemia), severe icterus, micro-clotting in anticoagulant tubes (EDTA, Citrate), or quantity not sufficient (QNS), rendering the sample analytically non-viable.
	The Laboratory maintains the clinical right and duty under ISO 15189 quality guidelines to reject compromised specimens and mandate a complimentary specimen re-collection.
	Analytical Measurement Uncertainty
	Every automated clinical chemistry, immunoassay, and hematology platform operates within recognized statistical measurement uncertainties. Reagents, calibration lot variations, and analyzer platforms vary across different diagnostic centers.
	Reference ranges are assay- and instrument-specific. Results generated by Sawariya Diagnostic Lab cannot be mathematically compared against reports from third-party laboratories utilizing alternative analytical methodologies.
	Assay Specificity & Cross-Reactivity
	Immunological and serological assays (e.g., viral antibodies, rapid immunochromatographic cards, chemiluminescent antigen assays) exhibit finite sensitivity and specificity. Cross-reacting antibodies or testing during the immunological "window period" can generate false positives or false negatives.
	Screening test findings must be clinically correlated and verified through confirmatory diagnostic modalities (e.g., molecular PCR, Western Blot, or serial testing) as directed by the treating physician.

4. Service Formation, Appointment Scheduling & Phlebotomy Operations


4.1 Enquiries vs. Confirmed Bookings
Submitting an appointment request, web form, phone query, or WhatsApp message constitutes an inquiry only and does not create a confirmed service booking or binding operational schedule. A booking is legally confirmed only when our administrative desk confirms test feasibility, verifies fasting compliance, issues a scheduled slot, and enters the booking into our central operational schedule.


4.2 Standard Phlebotomy & Procedural Consent
Venipuncture is an invasive clinical procedure. Presenting your arm or tendering a biological sample to our phlebotomist constitutes voluntary procedural consent. Our staff adhere strictly to aseptic standards, utilize sterile single-use vacuum collection tubes (vacutainers), and observe safe disposal protocols. Recognized minor procedural side effects—such as transient discomfort, localized subcutaneous hematoma (bruising), minor swelling, or vasovagal syncope (fainting)—can occur naturally during venipuncture, and the Laboratory bears no liability for such occurrences in the absence of actionable gross negligence.


4.3 Mobile Phlebotomy & Home Collection Standards
Home collection is an operational convenience service subject to technician availability, transit logistics, and environmental safety within designated zones of Charkhi Dadri and adjoining localities:
* Sanitary Domestic Environment: The requesting party must provide a clean, well-lit, safe physical environment free from unrestrained animals or domestic hazards.
* Zero-Tolerance Staff Protection Policy: Sawariya Diagnostic Lab enforces a strict zero-tolerance policy against physical intimidation, verbal abuse, sexual harassment, or unlawful detention of our visiting phlebotomists. Phlebotomists possess full institutional authority to immediately terminate a visit, refuse sample collection, and leave the premises if their physical safety or dignity is compromised. Any such incident will result in permanent service termination and immediate referral to local law enforcement authorities.
* Cold-Chain Maintenance: Biological specimens collected remotely are barcoded immediately upon venipuncture and transported in temperature-controlled, validated sample carriers to guarantee pre-analytical stability.

5. Laboratory Information System (LIS), Automation & Critical Value Protocols
5.1 Barcode Accessioning & Automated Interfacing
Every primary tube is labeled at accessioning with a unique linear or 2D machine-readable barcode encoding the patient ID. Where supported by our testing equipment (such as automated biochemistry analyzers, chemiluminescence immunoassay [CLIA] systems, automated hematology analyzers, and HPLC instruments), test requests and analytical results are transferred via bi-directional LIS data interfaces to eliminate manual data entry errors.


5.2 Quality Assurance & Pathologist Sign-Off
Our LIS utilizes automated delta checks (evaluating current results against historical patient values) and rule-based analytical checks to identify unexpected physiological anomalies. In compliance with applicable regulatory standards, diagnostic reports undergo verification and digital sign-off by a qualified consulting pathologist or biochemist before release.


5.3 Critical Panic Value Notification Protocol
When an analytical result falls outside physiological limits and exceeds defined life-threatening alert thresholds (e.g., severe hypoglycemia, profound thrombocytopenia, critical electrolyte imbalance, extreme leukocytosis), the Laboratory activates its emergency Critical Value Notification Protocol:
* The Laboratory will attempt prompt communication of the critical result via priority phone call, SMS, or WhatsApp dispatch to the patient's registered contact number or the referring physician.
* While every operational effort is made to convey critical alerts promptly, the Laboratory cannot guarantee that the call will be answered and disclaims liability for carrier connection failures, unreachable numbers, or the patient's delay in acting upon critical notifications.

10. Statutory Healthcare Compliance & Mandatory Disease Reporting


10.1 Notifiable Diseases Reporting
Under the Epidemic Diseases Act, 1897, the Disaster Management Act, 2005, and directives from the Ministry of Health and Family Welfare (MOHFW), the Indian Council of Medical Research (ICMR), and the Integrated Disease Surveillance Programme (IDSP), clinical laboratories are legally required to report confirmed positive results for statutorily declared Notifiable Diseases (e.g., Tuberculosis, Dengue, Malaria, Typhoid, Hepatitis, COVID-19, Swine Flu, HIV) to designated district surveillance officers and competent government health authorities.
Such mandatory disclosure is required by law, does not require prior patient consent, and does not constitute a breach of medical confidentiality or the DPDP Act, 2023.


10.2 Strict Adherence to the PC-PNDT Act, 1994
Sawariya Diagnostic Lab operates in strict compliance with the Pre-Conception and Pre-Natal Diagnostic Techniques (Prohibition of Sex Selection) Act, 1994 ("PC-PNDT Act"). The Laboratory does not perform, facilitate, or entertain any test, procedure, or inquiry directed toward pre-natal sex determination or sex selection. Any attempt or solicitation to utilize our services for unlawful pre-natal sex determination will be reported to district health enforcement authorities immediately.


10.3 Bio-Medical Waste Management
All biological samples, needles, lancets, vacutainers, and contaminated consumables are segregated, treated, and disposed of in strict accordance with the Bio-Medical Waste Management Rules, 2016, via state-authorized common bio-medical waste treatment operators.

11. The Patient Bill of Rights


Aligned with the National Human Rights Commission (NHRC) Charter of Patients' Rights and Ministry of Health and Family Welfare guidelines, Sawariya Diagnostic Lab guarantees every patient the following rights:
1. Right to Adequate Information: The right to clear, understandable details regarding test names, required pre-test fasting instructions, standard turnaround times (TAT), and applicable tariffs prior to collection.
2. Right to Access Diagnostic Records: The right to receive an authentic, signed diagnostic report physically or via authenticated digital channels upon settlement of service fees.
3. Right to Privacy and Dignity: The right to dignified, respectful, and hygienic treatment during sample collection and registration, ensuring privacy and modesty are safeguarded.
4. Right to Confidentiality: The right to have all diagnostic records, medical indications, and personal details protected from unauthorized disclosure, except where statutory public health reporting is mandated by law.
5. Right to Informed Consent & Refusal: The right to receive an explanation of invasive phlebotomy procedures and to decline testing or withdraw consent prior to specimen collection, with understanding of the diagnostic implications.
6. Right to Non-Discrimination: The right to receive equitable, high-standard diagnostic care regardless of caste, creed, religion, gender, sexual orientation, disability, socioeconomic status, or medical condition.
7. Right to Transparent Invoicing: The right to receive an itemized receipt detailing test charges and collection fees.
8. Right to Grievance Redressal: The right to submit complaints regarding specimen collection, reporting delays, billing discrepancies, or staff conduct, and to receive a timely, transparent administrative investigation.
