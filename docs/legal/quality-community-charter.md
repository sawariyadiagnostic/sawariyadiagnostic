1. Quality Policy, Regulatory Architecture & Scope of Governance
1.1 The Institutional Commitment
Sawariya Diagnostic Lab is committed to delivering accurate, timely, clinically correlated, and reproducible in-vitro diagnostic evaluations across clinical biochemistry, hematology, clinical pathology, immunoassay, serology, and microbiology. Our technical and administrative operations are structured in alignment with:
* The technical and competence standards of ISO 15189:2022 (Medical laboratories — Requirements for quality and competence);
* The Clinical Establishments (Registration and Regulation) Act, 2010 (and applicable State adaptations/amendments);
* The Digital Personal Data Protection Act, 2023 (DPDP Act);
* The Bio-Medical Waste Management Rules, 2016;
* The Consumer Protection Act, 2019;
* The National Human Rights Commission (NHRC) Charter of Patients’ Rights.


1.2 Administrative vs. Clinical Boundaries
This Charter delineates operational commitments and clinical safeguards. Public notices, informational brochures, or website catalog listings do not constitute an actionable guarantee of physiological cure, an exhaustive health assessment, or an independent clinical diagnosis. Diagnostic testing serves as a technical investigational tool provided strictly to assist qualified medical practitioners.

2.1 Internal Quality Control (IQC) Protocols
* Multi-Rule Evaluation: Automated clinical chemistry, immunoassay, and hematology analyzers are subjected to daily, multi-level control runs (normal, abnormal, and critical ranges) evaluated against Levey-Jennings control charts and standardized Westgard rules before patient sample testing commences.
* Calibration & Traceability: Automated analyzers (including CLIA systems, automated biochemistry analyzers, 3-part/5-part hematology counters, and HPLC systems) undergo scheduled multi-point calibration using traceable primary calibrators. Reagent lot verification and open-vial stability logs are systematically maintained.
2.2 External Quality Assessment Schemes (EQAS)
The Laboratory participates in recognized External Quality Assessment Schemes (EQAS) and inter-laboratory proficiency testing programs to benchmark analytical accuracy against state, national, and international peer laboratories. Proficiency test deviations are immediately routed into Corrective and Preventive Action (CAPA) workflows.

3. Pre-Analytical Integrity, Specimen Accessioning & Rejection Governance
Post-Analytical Specimen Retention & Reflex Testing Protocols
* Specimen Archival Window: Biological specimens are retained following test completion in monitored cold storage ($2^\circ\text{C}\text{ to }8^\circ\text{C}$) to facilitate re-examination or clinical verification:
   * Serum / Heparin Plasma Aliquots: Retained for forty-eight (48) hours.
   * EDTA Whole Blood Tubes: Retained for twenty-four (24) hours.
   * Stained Cytology / Blood Smear Slides: Retained for five (5) years.
* Add-On Test Requests: Requests by treating physicians for supplementary investigations on existing archived specimens are honored only if the requested analyte remains biochemically stable within the validated post-draw time window. If analyte degradation has begun, a fresh venipuncture draw is mandated.
Diagnostic reliability depends directly upon biological sample integrity. The Laboratory enforces strict accessioning criteria. Biological samples non-compliant with standard criteria are rejected to prevent erroneous reporting.


Parameter / Failure Mode
	Root Cause & Clinical Impact
	Mandatory Laboratory Action
	In-Vitro Hemolysis
	Traumatic venipuncture, needle shear, or thermal exposure causing erythrocyte lysis; causes spurious elevations of Potassium ($K^+$), LDH, AST, and interference in optical colorimetric assays.
	Immediate Specimen Rejection. Re-collection requested at zero additional fee to the patient.
	Gross Lipemia / Severe Icterus
	High lipid turbidity or hyperbilirubinemia interfering with optical, spectrophotometric, or turbidimetric analyzer sensors.
	Specimen evaluated for dilution protocol or high-speed clearing; rejected if analytical interference threshold is exceeded.
	In-Tube Micro-Clotting
	Inadequate inversion of anticoagulant vacutainers (K2/K3-EDTA or Sodium Citrate); artificially depletes platelet counts and skews hematocrit/CBC determinations.
	Immediate Specimen Rejection. Tube will not be analyzed; complimentary venipuncture re-draw executed.
	Quantity Not Sufficient (QNS)
	Inadequate draw volume violating anticoagulant-to-blood ratios (especially critical in Coagulation/PT-INR sodium citrate tubes).
	Sample flagged as QNS; discarded under bio-medical waste safety rules; secondary sample collection mandated.
	Pre-Analytical Fasting Violation
	Consumption of caloric food, caffeine, or supplements within 10–12 hours prior to draw for fasting panels (Lipid Profile, Fasting Plasma Glucose).
	Patient advised of metabolic distortion; sample draw rescheduled to protect clinical utility.
	Cold-Chain Interruption
	Exposure of temperature-sensitive samples (hormones, viral antigens, specialized enzymes) to ambient temperatures exceeding validated $2^\circ\text{C}$ to $8^\circ\text{C}$ thresholds during transit.
	Sample flagged for logistics failure; discarded; immediate replacement collection dispatched.

4. Turnaround Time (TAT) Architecture & Critical Value Escalation Protocol


4.1 Turnaround Time Commitments & Diagnostic Categories
Turnaround Time (TAT) is calculated from the physical arrival and LIS barcode accessioning of the specimen inside our testing facility until the verified report is digitally authorized.
* Routine Investigations (Biochemistry, CBC, Urinalysis): 4 to 8 hours.
* Automated Immunoassays (Thyroid panels, Fertility, Vitamin D, B12, CLIA markers): Same-day or within 12 to 24 hours.
* Specialized Outsourced Reference Investigations (Histopathology, Karyotyping, Specialized Genetic Assays): Defined by specialized reference partner schedules (typically 3 to 7 working days).
* TAT Extension Conditions: TAT commitments are operational benchmarks and may be extended when secondary confirmatory testing, sample dilution, delta check re-runs, or pathologist verification is required.

* Immediate Verification: The duty biochemist/pathologist re-runs the primary sample to rule out mechanical or pipetting anomalies.
* Rapid Direct Escalation: The administrative/clinical desk initiates priority communication via telephone or direct emergency dispatch to the referring physician or the patient's verified contact number within fifteen (15) to thirty (30) minutes of verification.
* Audit Trail Logging: All panic notifications are archived in the LIS Panic Log, recording the parameter, verified value, timestamp, caller name, recipient identity, and read-back confirmation.
* Limitation: The Laboratory disclaims liability where recipient phones are switched off, out of network coverage, or unattended by the patient or clinical team.
4.3 Representative Critical Panic Thresholds Schedule
While individual clinical limits are reviewed and calibrated by our Consulting Pathologist, the Laboratory enforces mandatory immediate escalation for the following primary critical parameters:
* Serum Potassium (): $< 2.8\text{ mmol/L}$ (Severe Hypokalemia — cardiac arrhythmia risk) or $> 6.2\text{ mmol/L}$ (Severe Hyperkalemia).
* Blood Glucose: 45\text{ mg/dL}$ (Neuroglycopenia / hypoglycemic crisis) or $> 400\text{ mg/dL}$ (Diabetic ketoacidosis / hyperosmolar state).
* Platelet Count: $< 20,000\text{ /µL}$ (Spontaneous hemorrhage risk).
* Hemoglobin (Adult): $< 6.0\text{ g/dL}$ (Severe tissue hypoxia).
* Cardiac Biomarkers (Troponin I / T): Qualitative / Quantitative positive above clinical decision limit for acute myocardial injury.
* Coagulation (PT / INR): $\text{INR} > 4.5$ (Critical coagulopathy / intracranial bleed risk).

5. Post-Analytical Governance, Report Verification & Anti-Tampering Standards


5.1 Qualified Medical Review & Digital Authentication
No diagnostic report is released into the public domain, LIS patient portal, or direct delivery channel without clinical verification and digital authorization by a certified consulting pathologist or biochemist possessing recognized qualifications under the National Medical Commission (NMC) or relevant statutory councils.


5.2 Post-Analytical Report Corrections & Amendments
To maintain clinical and legal audit integrity, diagnostic records are never erased, overwritten, or modified retrospectively:
* Amended Report Issuance: Any clinically validated correction (e.g., following specimen re-testing or demographic correction) is issued as an explicitly titled "Amended / Supplementary Report".
* Audit Trail Preservation: The amended document references the original accession number, states the exact reason for the amendment, logs the pathologist who authorized the amendment, and preserves the historical record in the permanent LIS database.


5.3 Anti-Tampering, Digital Verification & Fraud Prevention
Diagnostic reports issued by Sawariya Diagnostic Lab are legal clinical instruments. To prevent unauthorized manipulation:
* Reports incorporate unique machine-readable QR codes linking back to our secure, authenticated verification gateway.
* Reports feature anti-copy digital background watermarks and cryptographic signatures.
* Statutory Criminal Warning: Any unauthorized digital editing, photoshopping, numerical alteration, or forgery of a diagnostic report for insurance claims, employment certification, judicial proceedings, or visa applications constitutes a cognizable offense under the Bharatiya Nyaya Sanhita, 2023 and the Information Technology Act, 2000. Sawariya Diagnostic Lab aggressively prosecutes detected report forgery and immediately alerts administrative and law enforcement authorities.

6.1 Clinical Scope of Community Screenings
Community health camps provide preventive, baseline physiological screening and health education. They do not constitute emergency trauma units, mobile hospitals, or inpatient curative facilities. Screenings identify potential biological risk markers; participants with abnormal readings are directed to consult local government health centers (PHCs, CHCs), Civil Hospitals, or private medical practitioners.


6.2 Informed Consent & Transparent Communication
* Participation in health camps is entirely voluntary.
* Screenings require informed consent (verbal or written, presented in Hindi and English) outlining the screening scope, venipuncture procedure, and normal biological limits.
* For school health checkups or pediatric screenings, formal institutional authorization and parental/guardian consent protocols are observed.


6.3 Field Phlebotomy, Cold-Chain & Bio-Medical Waste in Camp Settings
* Cold-Chain Field Safeguards: Field collection teams adhere strictly to the same cold-chain storage standards as central facility phlebotomists. Samples are preserved in validated cool packs ($2^\circ\text{C}$ to $8^\circ\text{C}$) and transferred promptly to the central analyzer facility.
* Mobile Bio-Medical Waste Neutralization: Camp sites maintain dedicated puncture-proof sharps containers (translucent white/blue) and color-coded clinical waste bags. No contaminated consumables, needles, or biological waste are left behind at public, school, or rural sites. All waste is transported back to the central facility and handed over to the authorized common bio-medical waste disposal operator.


6.4 Non-Discriminatory Access & Tariff Transparency
Community health camps operate without discrimination based on caste, creed, religion, gender, disability, or socioeconomic background. All subsidized tariffs, charitable sponsorships, or package rates are disclosed publicly prior to screening.


6.5 Field Phlebotomy Safety, Syncope Management & Medical Referrals
All community health camp and rural outreach stations are equipped with dedicated recovery couches and oral rehydration supplies to manage transient vasovagal syncope, vertigo, or mild hematomas. Phlebotomy staff are trained in basic first aid. In the event of an acute medical situation during screening, field personnel immediately contact local government emergency health services (108) or facilitate transfer to the nearest Primary Health Centre (PHC) or Civil Hospital. Community health camps strictly provide screening data; field staff do not dispense prescription medication or alter clinical treatment regimens.

7. Patient Dignity, Respectful Phlebotomy & Data Confidentiality


7.1 Dignity, Privacy and Safe Phlebotomy Practices
* Professional Modesty: Phlebotomy draws are executed with absolute regard for patient privacy, modesty, and personal dignity. Dedicated, segregated collection spaces are provided for female patients and pediatric cases.
* Single-Use Consumables: Phlebotomists open sterile, sealed vacutainers, alcohol swabs, and needles in the direct visual presence of the patient or guardian.
* Vasovagal Safety: Phlebotomy stations are equipped with recumbent chairs or recovery couches to manage transient vasovagal syncope or dizziness.


7.2 Strict Data Confidentiality & DPDP Act Compliance
* Confidential Health Information: Patient demographic data, investigative findings, and clinical histories are confidential health records protected under the Digital Personal Data Protection Act, 2023.
* Role-Based Access Control (RBAC): Access within our LIS is partitioned. Technicians have access strictly to processing modules; clinical verification is restricted to pathologists; administrative staff access billing files without access to clinical histories.
* Prohibition of Third-Party Commercial Disclosures: Sawariya Diagnostic Lab strictly prohibits the sale, leasing, or commercial sharing of patient data with pharmaceutical marketers, insurance brokers, or external advertising platforms.


7.3 Communication Handoff Boundaries
While the Laboratory utilizes modern communication handoffs (WhatsApp Business API, automated SMS portals, cloud email) for the rapid delivery of reports, patients acknowledge that external telecommunication carriers govern transmission paths. Patients must secure their personal mobile devices and verify their contact numbers during registration.

8.1 Formal Redressal Escalation Protocol
* Step 1: Front-Desk / Operational Escalation: Minor concerns (billing questions, receipt reprints, delivery status) are handled directly by the Operations Manager at our central desk.
* Step 2: Formal Grievance Referral: Discrepancies regarding analytical results, collection conduct, privacy concerns, or unaddressed delays are submitted in writing or via email to the Statutory Quality and Grievance Officer.
* Step 3: Root-Cause Investigation: The Quality Officer coordinates with the laboratory technologist and reviewing pathologist to examine calibration logs, IQC runs for the batch in question, specimen accessioning images, and cold-chain transit logs.
* Step 4: Formal Determination & Resolution: The Laboratory provides a formal written response within fifteen (15) business days, detailing findings and offering appropriate remediation (such as complimentary re-testing, verified amendment, or refund).

9. Legal Disclaimers, Third-Party Providers & Liability Limitations


9.1 Professional Effort Standard
This Quality & Community Charter outlines our institutional standard of care and quality processes. Diagnostic medicine involves biological variations, assay sensitivities, and analytical measurement tolerances. This Charter does not constitute a warranty of absolute biological certainty or an assumption of strict liability.


9.2 Third-Party Specialized Referral Laboratories
For specialized investigations not performed on-site (such as advanced cytogenetics, mass spectrometry, or rare oncological markers), Sawariya Diagnostic Lab routes specimens to accredited external reference laboratories:
* Reports for outsourced tests clearly disclose the name and credentials of the processing reference facility.
* Sawariya Diagnostic Lab exercises standard due diligence in selecting accredited referral partners but is not liable for analytical delays, reagent shortages, or mechanical disruptions occurring at third-party reference facilities.
9.3 Limitation of Liability and Standard of Care
Sawariya Diagnostic Lab exercises reasonable professional skill, knowledge, and diligence consistent with prevailing diagnostic laboratory practices and NABL ISO 15189 principles. The Laboratory's findings reflect analytical determinations based on the specific biological sample submitted under particular pre-analytical conditions. To the extent permissible under the Consumer Protection Act, 2019, and applicable Indian law:
(a) The Laboratory disclaims liability for diagnostic distortions arising from undisclosed patient medication, non-compliance with fasting instructions, or physiological variations beyond analytical detection;
(b) In no event shall the Laboratory be liable for indirect, incidental, or consequential damages (including loss of business or personal distress); and
(c) Direct financial recovery for proven analytical non-conformity resulting solely from laboratory handling is limited to compensatory re-testing or reimbursement of the affected test fee.
