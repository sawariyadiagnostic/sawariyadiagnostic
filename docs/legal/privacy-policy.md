This Policy encompasses all touchpoints associated with our diagnostic operations, including:
* Physical patient interactions at our central processing laboratory in Charkhi Dadri, Haryana.

2. Information We Collect


In compliance with clinical standard operating procedures and data minimization principles, we collect information under five distinct operational classifications:
2.1 Patient Identification and Demographic Data
* Personal Identifiers: Full legal name, biological sex, age, date of birth, guardian name (for minor patients), and relationship details.
* Contact Coordinates: Primary mobile telephone number, alternate contact number, residential/home collection address, landmark coordinates, PIN code, and email address.
* Government and Scheme Identification: Masked Aadhaar number (last four digits only, where voluntarily submitted), Ayushman Bharat Health Account (ABHA ID), or relevant government healthcare scheme credentials necessary for subsidized or cashless public health processing.


2.2 Clinical, Diagnostic, and Specimen Data
* Physician and Clinical History: Name and registration credentials of the referring doctor, clinical prescription, suspected diagnoses, presenting clinical symptoms, and medication history affecting baseline clinical reference ranges (e.g., anticoagulant, insulin, or hormone therapy).
* Biological Specimen Metadata: Unique Laboratory Accession Number / Sample Identifier (SID), specimen matrix (e.g., whole blood, serum, plasma, urine, sputum, body fluids), collection timestamp, fasting state, and cold-chain transport temperature logs.
* Analytical Test Outputs: Raw instrument optical density (OD) readings, chemiluminescent immunoassay (CLIA) relative light units (RLU), hematological cell count metrics, calibrated quantitative/qualitative values, pathologist review annotations, delta check flags, and final signed clinical reports.


2.3 Logistics, Phlebotomy, and Sample Route Data
* Location and Geolocation Data: Address landmarks, GPS coordinates, and arrival instructions provided for dispatched phlebotomists.
* Chain-of-Custody Telemetry: Phlebotomist identification, vacutainer barcode scan records, cold-box seal confirmations, transit duration, and central lab accession receipt logs.


2.4 Billing, Transaction, and Corporate Data
* Payment Records: Transaction identifiers, UPI transaction references, point-of-sale (POS) receipts, cash vouchers, GST-compliant tax invoices, credit ledger records, and applied discount/package tiers.
* Institutional Sponsorship: Corporate wellness vouchers, employer health check-up authorizations, or Third-Party Administrator (TPA) settlement parameters.


2.5 Technical, Web, and Channel Interaction Logs
* Digital Communication Records: Inbound and outbound WhatsApp communication logs, automated dispatch timestamps, SMS delivery status receipts, and customer service call logs.
* Network and Device Data: Internet Protocol (IP) address, browser client architecture, operating system metadata, secure session cookies, and interface error logs.


2.6 Pediatric and Minor Patient Data
Diagnostic testing involving individuals under the age of 18 ("Minors") is undertaken strictly with the verifiable consent of a parent or legal guardian. In accordance with Section 9 of the DPDP Act, 2023, the Lab does not execute behavioral tracking, targeted health advertising, or profile mining on minor data. All pediatric diagnostic records are held in fiduciary custody and released solely to the verified guardian or authorized pediatrician.

4. Sharing and Disclosure of Information


Sawariya Diagnostic Lab does not sell, lease, monetize, or trade patient health records or personal identifiers. Data is shared strictly under the following controlled operational parameters:
* Treating Physicians and Clinical Consultants: Verified diagnostic reports are delivered to your referring medical practitioner, clinic, or hospital to ensure clinical continuity and timely treatment decisions.
* Accredited Reference Laboratories: When specialized esoteric, genetic, or specialized histopathology tests are required that exceed our central laboratory's on-site test menu, specimens and necessary clinical histories are transferred to accredited reference partner facilities under formal Data Processing Agreements (DPAs) and chain-of-custody protocols.
* Laboratory Information System & Cloud Infrastructure: We utilize enterprise software vendors for Laboratory Information System (LIS) hosting, cloud database storage, SMS gateways, and official WhatsApp Business API dispatch. All vendors operate under strict data protection covenants prohibiting secondary utilization of data.
* Public Health and Regulatory Bodies: Where mandated by statute, confirmed positive laboratory findings for notifiable diseases are transmitted to the Integrated Disease Surveillance Programme (IDSP) and District CMO authorities.
* Judicial and Law Enforcement Orders: We may disclose records in response to a lawful court summons, judicial order, or statutory warrant under the Bharatiya Nagarik Suraksha Sanhita (BNSS). Affected individuals will be notified unless prohibited by law.
* De-Identified and Aggregated Datasets: Completely de-identified, non-reversible epidemiological statistics (e.g., seasonal positivity rates for hemoglobinopathies or vector-borne antigens) may be aggregated for laboratory quality benchmarking or scientific research.

5. Clinical Record Immutability and Diagnostic Disclaimers


* Immutability of Verified Clinical Reports: A diagnostic report verified and digitally signed by our consulting pathologist is an official legal-medical document. In accordance with clinical accreditation standards, verified diagnostic records cannot be retroactively altered, expunged, or deleted on request. If a clinical re-evaluation or test rerun occurs, an official "Amended Supplementary Report" is issued, preserving the original test result and providing an unalterable audit log explaining the clinical adjustment.
* Digital Communication Security: Electronic report dispatch via WhatsApp, SMS, or Email provides accessible patient care. While our transport infrastructure is encrypted, end-user terminal privacy depends on your device security. You are responsible for maintaining custody of your phone number and downloaded files. Physical hard-copy reports remain available at our laboratory reception desk at all times.
* Medical Advisory Disclaimer: Laboratory test findings reflect analytical values of the specific specimen submitted at a given time. Analytical values must be evaluated in conjunction with clinical symptoms, medical history, and physical examination by a registered physician. Laboratory reports do not constitute independent prescriptions, medical diagnoses, or therapeutic directives.

6. Technical Security and Data Safeguards


Sawariya Diagnostic Lab implements technical, organizational, and physical safeguards designed to prevent unauthorized access, data loss, or breach:
* Data Encryption Standards: All digital web transit and API endpoints utilize Transport Layer Security (TLS 1.3). LIS database backups and archived records are encrypted at rest using Advanced Encryption Standard (AES-256).
* Role-Based Access Control (RBAC): Technical access is compartmentalized. Front-desk receptionists are limited to demographic and billing modules; phlebotomists access sample collection queues; laboratory technicians access instrument entry portals; only authorized pathologists possess cryptographic signing keys to validate diagnostic reports.
* Physical Laboratory Perimeter Security: Central processing analyzer suites, cold-storage units, and server consoles are housed within controlled-access zones protected by continuous CCTV monitoring and physical authorization checks.
* Comprehensive Audit Trails: The LIS captures immutable, time-stamped audit logs for every record creation, test modification, report view, download, or printing event.
* Breach Notification Protocol: In the event of a confirmed personal data breach posing risk to Data Principals, the Lab will notify the Data Protection Board of India (DPBI) and affected individuals in full accordance with the DPDP Act, 2023.

8. Patient Choices and Statutory Rights


Under the Digital Personal Data Protection Act, 2023, you are entitled to specific rights regarding your personal and medical data:
* Right to Access & Summary: You may request a verified summary of your personal data, diagnostic test history, and details of third parties with whom your records have been shared.
* Right to Correction & Updating: You may request correction of inaccurate, misleading, or outdated personal demographic coordinates (e.g., updating an incorrect contact number or correcting a misspelled name).
* Right to Erasure (Subject to Clinical Limitations): You may request deletion of non-statutory personal data. Note on Clinical Exception: The right to erasure does not apply to verified diagnostic reports, biological accession numbers, or tax invoices that the Lab is legally obligated to preserve under NMC regulations, the Clinical Establishments Act, and tax statutes.
* Right to Nominate: In accordance with Section 14 of the DPDP Act, you have the right to nominate an individual who, in the event of your death or incapacity, shall exercise your rights with respect to your medical records.
* Right to Withdraw Consent: Where processing relies purely on optional consent (e.g., receiving annual preventive health reminders), you may withdraw consent at any time without affecting past lawful processing.
Data Categories Available for Direct Export / Portability Upon Verified Request:
* Cumulative diagnostic test summaries and verified pathology reports (PDF / structured format).
* Master demographic profile and registered emergency contact listings.
* Itemized GST billing statements and payment transaction receipts.
* Recorded digital consent authorizations.
